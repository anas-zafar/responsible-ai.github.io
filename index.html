Who is responsible?

  • The data? Representativeness, quality, consent, and what leaks back out through memorization.
  • The models? Bias, hallucination, robustness, and failures that appear only when models use tools.
  • The users? Misuse, jailbreaks, deepfakes, and the human oversight that high-stakes use requires.
  • The regulations? The EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, and ISO/IEC 42001, and the evidence each one asks for.

Abstract

Generative AI is rapidly moving from research to deployment, elevating the need for responsible development, evaluation, and governance.

We conduct a PRISMA-guided review of studies published between November 2022 and December 2025, spanning large language models, vision-language models, diffusion models, and agentic pipelines. The survey bridges governance principles, technical evaluation, and domain deployment, and it scores major AI safety benchmarks against a ten-criterion rubric paired with a policy crosswalk to regulatory requirements.

Three findings emerge: benchmark coverage is dense for bias and toxicity but sparse for privacy, provenance, deepfakes, and system-level failures in agentic settings; evaluations remain largely static and task-local, limiting audit portability; and inconsistent documentation complicates cross-release comparison. The study offers a structured path to align the evaluation of generative AI with the governance needs for safe and responsible deployment.

  • Vision-language models
  • Diffusion models

What the survey contributes

  1. One survey across governance, evaluation, and deployment. It synthesizes the NIST AI RMF, the EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001, and related instruments for all four system types.

  2. A ten-criterion rubric (C1 to C10). It scores widely used AI safety benchmarks on risk-surface coverage and assurance quality, with a policy crosswalk that maps each benchmark to the governance requirements it can evidence.

  3. Twelve lifecycle KPIs. These come with explainability guidance for foundation models and a catalogue of AI-ready testbeds for continuous evaluation.

  4. Domain analysis. The same lens is applied to healthcare, finance, education, arts, agriculture, and defense.

Three findings

Coverage is uneven

Benchmarks are dense for bias and toxicity but sparse for privacy, provenance, deepfakes, and system-level failures in agentic settings.

Evaluation is static

Most evaluations are static and task-local, so their evidence does not carry over to audits or lifecycle assurance.

Documentation is inconsistent

Uneven documentation and metric validity make it hard to compare results across releases and domains.

The C1 to C10 rubric

Each benchmark is scored 0, 1, or 2 on every criterion by two independent reviewers. C1 to C8 map the risk surface; C9 and C10 assess how usable the evidence is for governance. The coverage notes below are the patterns the survey reports across the benchmarks it scored.

Twelve lifecycle KPIs

The KPIs turn responsible AI goals into metrics that can be tracked, compared, and audited across the model lifecycle. The paper links each one to benchmarks, datasets, and audit toolkits.

  1. Data quality and integrity
  2. Data privacy compliance
  3. Bias detection (statistical parity difference)
  4. Fairness ratio (disparate impact)
  5. Equal performance (accuracy parity)
  6. Robustness assessment
  7. Explainability index
  8. Local faithfulness gap
  9. High-stakes error rate
  10. Audit frequency and resolution time
  11. User inclusivity index
  12. Sustainability (energy and emissions)
